# General

## FAQs

### What is a Hackathon?

Hackathon is a state of mind.

A hackathon is a fast-paced event, where participants team up to create solutions and concepts from scratch in a short amount of time, addressing current problems. There are hackathons of various lengths and types, but one common characteristic of them is that they are centered around a specific theme. This year’s theme is European Tech Renaissance.

Usually, the teams that perform the best in hackathons are the ones with the most diverse set of members, ranging from techies and programmers to business and design enthusiasts. The most feasible prototypes are not only well functioning, but also well designed with a working business model.

### What is Junction?

Junction is a global tech community, organizing hackathons and tech events around the year and around the world offline and online. Junction 2026 is our flagship event - Europe’s leading hackathon, organized for the 11th time this year!

### What is happening at the Junction 2026 event?

Junction 2026 is a weekend-long hackathon, where participants team up and create tech projects to the challenges set by our partner companies. The projects can range from physical prototypes to new games, software and concepts. With their projects, participants also compete for winning prizes on a challenge, as well as the Main Prize of 10 000 EUR. The total prize pool of the event is 100 000 EUR.

### Do I need a team to attend?

Attending without a team is also possible! However, you can check the Junction Discord channel to find team members.

### Does it cost to attend?

Nope! Moreover, we provide drinks & snacks.

### Who are the people behind Junction?

Junction is run by a team of 8 full-timers and hundreds of volunteers, all passionate about organizing the best hackathons in the world!

### How are the winners chosen?

The winners of the challenge tracks are chosen by the companies providing the challenges. The finalists of Junction 2026 will be chosen with a peer-review system. The top projects will be pitched live for judges on stage on Sunday (15th).

### Can I claim the badge to a team member?

No, everyone has to claim their badges individually at the registration.

Public transportation journeys can be paid inside transportation vehicles by contactless payment with credit/debit card.

Public transportation tickets can be purchased from ticket machines at the transport stations and at the airport by the train station. There are also multiple places to purchase tickets, e.g. all R-Kioski shops.

Overall, public transportation in Helsinki area is very reliable and mostly on time. Do notice that the metro stops running around midnight. During the night you can grab a night bus or a taxi. Taxis in Finland are a reliable option, although a little bit more expensive.

**The team size can be 1-4 people**

Here’s how it works:

### Junction Platform: Team building

All team-related actions are happening on the Junction Platform. Open the Junction 2026 event on your profile’s dashboard and click the “Team” tab from the left bar.

1. **If you already have a team:**  Head to the Junction 2026 event on your profile’s dashboard in **Junction Platform**  and open the “Team” tab from the left bar. 1. Join the #teambuilding channel.

## Reviewing

### Evaluation criteria

#### Innovation

- Is the idea/concept/prototype new or different compared to the already presented ones?
- Does the idea/concept/prototype or some parts of it distinguish somehow from the already presented ones?

#### Impact

- Does the idea/concept/prototype have a significant impact on society through solving the problem(s) it addresses?
- Does the idea/concept/prototype have a significant impact on the majority of the people facing the problem it solves - how large is the ratio between the number of people whose problem got solved compared to the total people affected by the problem?

#### Implementation

- Is the idea/concept/prototype eventually realistic, viable and feasible to be implemented from all perspectives?

After submitting the projects, partner and peer reviewing will start in parallel. **All hackers need to be present at the venue for the live peer reviewing on Sunday (15th), 10:30–14:00.**

To ensure quality results, each participant should review the projects assigned to them. In case the team pitching are not at their assigned table, you will have the opportunity to  press the "not present" button. **If you or your team do not take part in the peer reviewing, you are not eligible for the main prize.**

Partners select the challenge winners and runner-ups.

The overall TOP 5 projects are determined based on the peer reviewing. TOP teams are instructed for the pitches in the closing ceremony for 3 **minutes**  each and afterwards there will be 3 **minutes for questions** , then **our panel of 7 judges vote on the best projects**

**You can recognize us easily by the vests we wear:**

- **Community service officers (security) – supervisors: Orange high-visibility vests**

If you need first aid or any other kind of medical assistance, please come to the **first aid station located on the 3rd floor** . If you have any medication that requires refrigeration, we have a fridge available for this at the first aid station.

You are free to exit and enter the venue during the event, but only through the official entrances. Do not use the emergency exits unless there is a real emergency. The emergency exit doors have alarms that will trigger when opened.

Please remember that when entering and inside the venue, you must always have your badge visible. If given any instructions by the community service officers or other event staff, you must obey their instructions.

### When to call the Junction security hotline:

- If you have a non-urgent matter or questions regarding security or first aid
- If you spot a fire that you were able to extinguish yourself
- Disturbances where no one is in danger: power outages etc.
- If you suspect a crime is happening
- If you or someone else is lost
- You’ve gotten sick and don’t know where to go

Try to call the hotline first, EXCEPT if:

- Someone’s life, health, property or the environment is threatened or in danger, or if you have reason to assume this to be the case

In these cases, call straight to the emergency number, and after that to the hotline so we know to guide the emergency services to the right place.

In Finland, we only use one emergency number, **112** . You can call the emergency number free of charge from any phone with no need for an area code. You can also call from a foreign mobile phone connection. You still won’t need an area code, just dial 112.

We recommend downloading the “112 Suomi” app from your app store. The ERC operator automatically obtains your location when calling through the app. This way you speed up the emergency services and you don’t need to know your exact location.

### How to call 112:

1. Make the call yourself if you can Call from a safe place
2. Explain what happened
3. Tell your exact address and municipality Hybridiarena Hype, Vanha Jorvaksentie 3, 02320 Espoo
4. Answer all the ERC operator’s questions
5. Follow the instructions given
6. Don’t end the call until permitted to

### What to do at the scene of an accident:

1. Find out what happened
2. Rescue anyone whose life is in danger
3. Move anyone injured to a safe place
4. Prevent further accidents
5. Warn others
6. Report the emergency to 112
7. Make sure that those injured are breathing and that their heart is beating
8. Stop any major bleeds
9. Continue to give first aid
10. Help the rescue workers find their way to the scene

## Code of Conduct

Junction stands for inclusivity. We believe that every single person has the right to participate in our events in a safe and welcoming environment.

Be respectful. Harassment and abuse are never tolerated in Junction events. If you are in a situation that makes you uncomfortable at any Junction event, if the event itself is creating an unsafe or inappropriate environment, or if interacting with a Junction representative or event organizer makes you uncomfortable, please report it using the procedures included below.

**Harassment includes but is not limited to:**

- Disrespectful or offensive verbal or written comments related to gender, age, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, race, religion, social class or economic status
- Deliberate violent or intimidating behaviour, such as stalking, following, harassing photography or recording
- Inappropriate physical contact and unwelcome sexual attention

If what you’re doing is making someone feel uncomfortable, that counts as harassment and is enough reason to stop doing it.

By attending a Junction event, you agree to obey these rules. Participants asked to stop any harassing behavior are expected to comply immediately. Sponsors, partners, mentors, volunteers, organizers, Junction staff, and anyone else at the event are also subject to the anti-harassment policy.

If an attendee engages in harassing behavior, Junction may take any action it deems appropriate, including issuing warning or expulsion from the event with no eligibility for reimbursement or refund of any type.

Junction representatives will be happy to help participants, provide escort, or otherwise help attendees feel safe for the duration of the event. We value your attendance. We expect all attendees to follow these rules at all event venues, as well as online interactions in relation to the event.

## Junction Rules

Here are the official rules for Junction 2026. To be eligible for prizes, including the **10 000 €**  main prize (total prize pool **100 000 €** ), you must follow these rules:

- No code written before the event.
- Allowed team size is 1 – 4.
- You must participate in at least one challenge.
- You and your team must take part in the live peer reviewing on Sunday to be eligible for the main prize.
- The project submission must contain a pitch video, maximum 2 minutes long.
- You must submit a link to your source. All code outside of open source libraries must be written at the event. If you use any code written specifically for this idea in advance, or work on a new project that is somehow related to an already existing company, you will be disqualified. All project submissions are licensed under the MIT open-source license, and organizers must be able to access your source code.
